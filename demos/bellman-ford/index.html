DSC 40B – Theoretical Foundations of Data Science II

Bellman-Ford

Instructions: Choose a graph, then click the buttons (or press the ← and → keys) to step through Bellman-Ford. Each step is one call to update.

The number next to each node is its estimated distance from the source, est, and the purple arrows point to each node from its estimated predecessor. The edge being updated is drawn in bold. One iteration updates every edge once; Bellman-Ford without early stopping runs |V| − 1 iterations in total.

Remember that the order in which Bellman-Ford updates the edges is arbitrary, in the sense that the algorithm is guaranteed to find the correct shortest paths no matter what order the edges are updated in. However, if we use Bellman-Ford with early stopping, some orders of updates may converge to the correct distances faster than others. The order that the edges are updated in the demo is shown in the status line above the graph.

Bellman-Ford never knows which of its estimates are correct: nothing in est says whether a number is final. What it does guarantee is the loop invariant: after k iterations, every node whose actual shortest path from the source has at most k edges is estimated correctly. Check Mark correct estimates under Options to see which estimates are already correct at each step, which is something the algorithm itself can't see.
